# Day 0 — Thursday, May 28, 2026

### The leaving — Sunnyvale → SJC → off to Japan

*The departure day. Family of five: Ajay, Candice, Zara (9), Kai (7), Popo (75). Reflected on from home and the airport, reconstructed from Albee captures. Times are Pacific (the device was on California time) — this is the day before Japan.*

**Source data:** ~196 moments · ~9.7 h of audio · ~36.6k words of transcription (Albee captures, Pacific time — night-before packing + the departure morning, recording ending at the SJC gate). Day 1 (arrival) is uncaptured — see `journals/day-1-2026-05-29.md`.

> **A note on the record:** Day 0 (the departure) is well captured. **Day 1 — the arrival in Tokyo on May 29 — was not recorded at all:** the recorder stayed off across the ~11-hour Zipair flight and the jet-lagged arrival, and the next capture is Day 2's Harajuku morning. So this journal covers the leaving; the landing lives only in memory.

## At a glance

The plan was simple and enormous: get five people, a grandmother, and a pile of bags out of the house and onto a Zipair flight to Tokyo for ten days.

What actually happened: a chaotic, loving, slightly frayed departure morning — final packing, a breakfast that doubled as a **rhyming bear-adventure story about the whole airport day**, a sibling-squabble that pushed Ajay to half-joke *"I don't know if we should go to Hawaii"*, coin pouches handed out for spending money in Japan, the drive to SJC, Zipair check-in, and a one-hour gate delay where the recording trails off. Then the flight, the dateline, and an arrival no one captured.

**The lesson of the day, in one line:** the trip starts before the trip — the rituals that carried all ten days (the morning walks, the games, the patience) were already being rehearsed at the kitchen table.

## The day, the way it happened

### The night before · ~May 27 evening (PDT)
The captures open the evening before, mid-pack: chargers and cables triaged on the floor — *"I'm bringing this power port because it has six USB ports from one charger"* — snacks sorted, a little late-night robot banter, the quiet arithmetic of fitting a family of five into a finite number of bags.

### Getting out the door · ~07:00–09:00 (PDT)
The departure morning was equal parts tenderness and friction. Haircut snips, toothbrushes packed, *"your pants are backwards,"* the recorder ("the nest") being set up for the trip. Popo made avocado toast; Ajay made strawberry-pancake plates and counted the blackberries out evenly between the kids.

Two beats stand out. The first is pure magic: over breakfast, Ajay narrated the entire day to come as a **rhyming bear adventure**, weaving the real instructions into the rhyme so the airport wouldn't be scary —

> *"Bags on belt, then step right in, telling rides inside a bin. Beep — don't worry, little cub, just put those tracks into this tub… Airplane leaves at half past four, lots of time left, let's explore… grab a salmon for the plane, then hop aboard the Bear Fort train."*
>
> *"Big bags up, small bags below, seat belts click and off we go. We wait and wait, and lastly push back from the gate. Down the runway, engines roll…"*

The second is the honest underside of family travel, said out loud while the kids bickered over breakfast: **"You are being so bad to each other. It's going to make us not want to go on these types of holidays. I booked one for Hawaii, and I don't know if we should go. This is not fun for us."** The exhaustion that bookends every big trip, captured before it even began.

Amid it, the small acts that set the tone for everything after: **coin pouches handed out** — *"we need to get you guys coin pouches so you have your own money to spend in Japan"* — and the ritual that would become the heart of the trip, planned in advance: *"Morning walk with Kai. Morning walk with Zara."* (Candice's version: *"I'm just going to shop."*)

### Drive, check-in, and the gate · ~09:00–11:30 (PDT)
Loading the cars (move the van, pull in the Volvo, "Kai, you're standing in the street — on the sidewalk"), the Uber XL, and the kids' dawning excitement: **"We're going to a Zipair." "To Japan, sir." "Are we at the airport?" "Yes, Zara." "Really?!"** At check-in, the bag math (four bags, five tickets, the value-package question) and the gentle warning that would prove very true: **"You're changing times — it's like one day, so it'll be more than a day. Let's try our best to sleep on the plane."** The last capture is at the gate: *"Are we at the right gate? Delayed an hour."* And then quiet — the flight, the dateline, and Tokyo, all unrecorded.

## What we learned about how *we* travel

The trip's DNA was visible on Day 0 (see [[travel-patterns]]):

1. **The rituals were chosen before the trip.** The morning one-on-one walks — the source of the best moments on Days 2 and 5 — were named at the kitchen table on departure day. Plan them in; they're not accidental.
2. **Give the kids their own money early.** Coin pouches on Day 0 turned "can I have" into "I'll spend mine," and front-loaded the autonomy that the souvenir-asking spiral later tested.
3. **Departure friction is normal — and it's not a verdict on the trip.** The "I don't know if we should go to Hawaii" moment came *before* the best ten days of the year. Expect the leaving to be hard; don't read it as an omen.
4. **Pre-frame scary logistics as a story.** The rhyming bear-airport narration is the same move as Pokémon-GO-as-walking-game: turn an ordeal (security, boarding) into an adventure the kids opt into.
5. **Respect the dateline.** *"It's like one day, so it'll be more than a day."* The arrival day (Day 1) vanished into jet lag and went unrecorded — proof that the day you land is a write-off. Plan nothing for it.
