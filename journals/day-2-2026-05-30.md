# Day 2 — Saturday, May 30, 2026

### Harajuku — the day the patterns were born

*Tokyo, the first big exploring day. Family of five: Ajay, Candice, Zara (9), Kai (7), Popo (75). Reflected on from the hotel, ~22:52 JST, reconstructed from Albee captures across the day.*

**Source data:** ~275 moments · ~13.6 h of audio · ~69.2k words of transcription (Albee captures, 05:56–22:52 JST).

---

## At a glance

The plan was a full Harajuku day: a dawn walk, Meiji Shrine, a meet-the-pigs animal café reservation, Takeshita-Street shopping, and the Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building light show after dark.

What actually happened: a long, golden, one-on-one Papa-and-Kai morning walk (with a running economics seminar), the shrine and its rituals, the pig café (the kids went from terrified to delighted), hours of Takeshita-Street gachapon and "look, don't touch," an afternoon retreat to the hotel for naps and laundry — and then an **evening that fell apart**: the light show missed, a dinner hunt that spiraled through half of Shinjuku before landing on a so-so dumpling counter, and card machines that wouldn't take Ajay's card at the train gates.

Reading it back now, Day 2 is the **Rosetta Stone of the whole trip** — almost every pattern we'd later name out loud first showed up here: one-on-one time, graze-don't-book, the souvenir-asking spiral, the pork-dinner problem, the cash/IC-card friction, and Pokémon GO as the family's connective tissue.

**The lesson of the day, in one line:** the morning we let unfold one-on-one was magic; the evening we left unplanned collapsed — *"this did not work tonight, we're going to look up restaurants in advance and have a plan."*

## The day, the way it happened

### The dawn walk · ~05:56–07:50
Ajay and Kai slipped out before the others into a quiet Shinjuku morning — and it became the template for the trip's best stretches. A konbini breakfast crawl (a coconut bun, a boiled egg Kai cracked himself, pudding, jelly, flan: *"I got flan for breakfast — that's crazy-baisy"*), a hunt for a closed park, and a steady stream of teaching. The biggest was money: **"Before money there were farmers and shepherds… they had to barter. So they made money so you can trade your work for paper. Gold is just a rock — it's only worth something because people believe it's worth something."** And, when Kai kept asking to come back together with Mama and Zara but worried they'd argue, Ajay gave the dynamic its name: *"You like undivided attention. You could just say — I like one-on-one time."* The phrase that would echo all the way to Hakone.

He also set up the day's other quiet constant — the recorder: *"My main phone is my Albee phone, that's its primary job."* When Zara later wanted to take it off, he made the case in one line: **"You'd never remember that whole conversation about money if I wasn't capturing it."**

### Meiji Shrine · ~07:50–09:10
The family reunited and walked into the shrine forest. Kai's wish, written on a card: **"I wish that my family was always alive. Love, Kai."** — paired with a gentle lesson about not living inside the wish: *"If you only focus on that one thing, you won't enjoy what's in front of you."* They learned the ritual (*"bow twice, put money in, clap, bow"*), marveled at the manicured trees, and clocked the culture: **"People wear suits to work every day here. That's crazy. Every country has a different culture — culture means how people agree to behave together."** And the attention math that explains so much: **"You can't do undivided attention with five people — it's always divided. It has nothing to do with you as a person."**

### Pokémon GO is born · ~09:13
A small, historic moment: Ajay introduced the game that would carry the next four days. *"I'll show you, but we'll do it at dedicated times, not all day. See, we just left that park — there are special Pokémon there. As you walk around, there might be Pokémon to pick up."* A flick, a catch — *"I got a Flabébé!"* — and the trip's most reliable tool was live.

### The pig café · ~09:30–10:30
Takeshita Street, a locker for shoes, hand sanitizer, and then a wall of small pigs. Kai, at the door: **"I'm scared of the pigs."** Two minutes later everyone was buried in them — *"Oh my gosh, they climb all over you… the baby pig, so cute."* The trick of the day, learned by trial: cradle them and they nuzzle into your arm (*"it's like sandpaper on my arm"*); hold them wrong and they bolt. "Hold the butt, hold the butt." They left with a brochure souvenir and a kid who'd flipped from fear to *"I want a pet pig."*

### Takeshita Street · ~10:30–12:10
The sensory overload of Harajuku: gachapon capsule machines where everyone twisted out a prize (*"whoa, he's got an axe and a machine gun — that's a really good one, buddy"*), candied fruit on sticks, sample stands, otter and capybara and dog cafés glimpsed through windows. Two threads that would recur all trip ran hard here. First, **the souvenir-asking spiral** — Kai cycling through wants, Ajay holding the line: *"Stop asking me over and over for things. Earlier you asked for the jelly, then the rocks, then the cone. That's a no."* Second, **a want done right**: Kai's quest for a little mist spray-bottle to clip on and spritz himself, finally found at Daiso — **"That's the one, buddy! Bubble spray bottle. Perfect."** A small repair in the middle: Zara's nosebleed, handled calmly against a wall — *"head down, drink some water, you're good."*

### Hotel rest · ~12:10–17:00
The afternoon pulled back: more Cat-Street browsing, then home to the hotel to do laundry and let the kids nap. (The nap is why Kai spent the rest of the night insisting it was "two days" — *"You took a nap, that's why you think it's two days. It's one day, buddy."*) Pokémon GO and quiet play filled the late afternoon.

### The evening that fell apart · ~19:35–22:52
Out again after dark for the Tokyo Metropolitan light show — and almost nothing went to plan. They missed the 8:30 show, drifted through Kabukichō to the giant **Godzilla Head**, and then the dinner hunt began in earnest: a yakiniku all-you-can-eat that didn't work (**"all the food can eat — except pork. We don't eat pork"** — Candice's constraint, surfacing for the first time), a teppan place with "nothing she'd want," and finally a food-court dumpling counter where most of it was pork and only Kai really ate. Card machines refused Ajay's card at the gates, so they walked home — a long, footsore trek with piggyback rides and heel-to-toe coaching. Out of it came the resolution that would shape every following day: **"We're going to look up restaurants in advance. We're going to have a plan, because this did not work tonight. And I have to get cash — I want lots of cash on me so I'm not stuck in this problem."** Home, showers, a goofy bedtime mind-reading game (*"What am I thinking?" "Pizza!" "Yes!"*), and sleep.

## What we learned about how *we* travel

These are the *origins* of patterns that later days confirmed (see [[travel-patterns]]):

1. **One-on-one beats five-way.** The dawn Papa-Kai walk was the day's best stretch; the explicit insight — *"you can't do undivided attention with five people"* — is why. Build deliberate 1:1 pairs into days. (Confirmed again Day 5's mountain walk.)
2. **Pre-lock dinner — the evening proved why.** No plan + Candice can't do pork + tired kids + card failures = a collapsed night. Ajay said the fix out loud here first: look up a (pork-safe) restaurant in advance, every day.
3. **Carry cash; don't rely on the card.** Card machines failed at the gates and the night turned into a long walk home. *"I have to get cash so I'm not stuck in this problem."* (Still unresolved days later.)
4. **Graze, don't book.** The konbini breakfast crawl and "food court, not a sit-down" instinct worked all morning. Flexible grazing fits the kids; the one big timed plan (the light show) is exactly what got missed.
5. **Expect the souvenir-asking spiral — pre-empt it.** The "look, don't touch" / "can I get this" loop ran nonstop in Harajuku. What worked: name a rule ("one souvenir at the end, after all the stores"), give a real want a real outcome (the spray bottle), and hold the line calmly.
6. **Turn transit/waiting into a game.** Pokémon GO debuted here; even I-spy and the bedtime mind-reading game defused the competition. Have a game ready.
7. **Kai is great one-on-one, dysregulated in the group.** The root of the sibling-competition tax that peaked on Day 4. The earlier you split and rotate, the better.
