# Day 3 — Sunday, May 31, 2026

### Asakusa → Skytree Town · the day we stopped rushing

*Tokyo. Family of five: Ajay, Candice, Zara (9), Kai (7), Popo (75). Reflected on from the hotel, ~20:30 JST, reconstructed from Albee captures across the day.*

---

## At a glance

The plan was a packed East-Tokyo arc: Senso-ji → Kappabashi → up the Skytree deck → Sumida Aquarium → train to Ikebukuro → biggest Pokémon Center → Sky Circus. We did maybe a third of it, and it was the best day yet.

What actually happened: a slow hotel morning, Senso-ji and omikuji fortunes, then we let the kids loose at a riverside playground, made our own onigiri for lunch, turned the walk to the Skytree into a Pokémon-GO hunt, found the Pokémon Store inside Skytree Town, and then mostly... stopped. Ajay found a spot to lie down and said out loud, *"this is the nicest part of my day — please don't ruin it by rushing to toys."* We watched clouds, played catch with a Charizard bottle, found a hammock, and let the afternoon be quiet. We never went up the Skytree, never made it to the aquarium, and never went to Ikebukuro. Nobody minded.

**The lesson of the day, in one line:** the itinerary is a menu, not a checklist — and the unscheduled hours were the ones we'll remember.

## The day, the way it happened

### Slow hotel morning · ~05:41–06:40
We woke up slowly. Packing the day-pack — three battery packs ("Albee running in my backpack all day"), passport into the locked pocket, Zara's hat, sunblock. Kai on the potty narrating. A Pixel buzzing at 6am like a phantom alarm. Strawberry milk turned into a "secret potion." There'd been a small argument the night before, named and patched gently in the morning ("I just want to tell you the truth… that's okay, I know"). Then, at the door: *"Day 3. Starting out."*

### Out the door · ~06:40–07:50
First stop was cash and a konbini breakfast — chocolate croissants and a hot latte; the kids rejected the breakfast sushi. At Yotsuya the Suica situation resolved itself only partway: **three working cards, one dead.** We tapped through on the working ones and decided to sort the broken card later rather than detour. Onto the Marunouchi line, transfer toward the Ginza line, kids chanting *"Let's go!"* the whole platform. Ajay pulled up the trip app on the train — *"I built this app just for Japan"* — and previewed Asakusa for them.

### Asakusa & Senso-ji · ~07:50–09:00
We arrived early enough to beat the buses. Kai had a hard morning meltdown ("I'm getting so angry I'm about to explode") and a lot of patient coaching about not being destructive. Then the day's first beautiful stretch:

- **The Earth talk.** Walking up toward the temple, Ajay explained the whole origin of everything to the kids — the Big Bang, a star exploding, dust clumping under gravity into stars and planets, Earth landing *"just lucky, far enough from the sun."* Kai: *"What if we fly onto the star?"* Then, quietly: *"do you want to take a picture of anything you see right now, just to remember that you were here?"* — paired a minute later with *"you're not going to remember you were here when you were bigger, because your memory will change."*
- **Omikuji at Senso-ji.** Drop a coin, shake the box, read the stick. The kids drew fortunes — **98, 86, 93** — and learned the clap-clap-bow. A real family ritual moment.
- Nakamise stalls, Hoppy Street, the golden temple roof ("where's the gold tower?"), souvenir browsing. Ajay split off briefly to get cash and circle back.

### The riverside playground · ~09:00–10:00
Here's where the plan got abandoned in the best way. Instead of Kappabashi, we found a playground/play area by the river ("the ugliest river I have ever seen — industrial and gray," but the kids didn't care). **Bumper boats in a pool, an obstacle course, slides, pedal-karts** ("14! 14!"). Apple juice, a scraped shin, the recurring Zara-bathroom negotiation. Pure unstructured kid joy.

### Make-your-own onigiri · ~10:00–11:50
Lunch beat the plan, too. We skipped the sukiyaki institution and found a **make-your-own-onigiri restaurant** — rice in a mold, fold it, fill it, squeeze it. *"It's an activity."* Salmon or chicken sets, hand-cut seaweed, tofu, a sour pickled plum, miso soup the kids were told was *"made by hand — they are artists,"* a little dessert and hot tea. Kai, unprompted: *"I love this restaurant."* Everyone ate together, which was the whole point. (Ajay also mused aloud about a product idea — restaurants always want a review; what if there were a better way to capture the good experiences.)

### Pokémon-GO walk to the Skytree · ~11:50–12:10
Light rain. To get five people walking happily toward the Skytree with no complaints, we opened Pokémon GO — *"tap the rhino," "there's a Squirtle," "Eevee," "that's a raid."* *"They forget that they're walking."* Best transit hack of the trip so far.

### Skytree Town / Solamachi · ~12:10–13:20
Not the observation deck — the base. A little fruit-and-snack market (figs, watermelon, snow crab samples, iced lattes; *"we have to buy something so we can sit here"*). Then the toy floors, hunting for the Pokémon Store across eight floors of mall — and the payoff: **"That's the Pokémon Store! See, I told you — when you find it, you find it!"** Kai chose a **Baby Charizard** and some **Pokémon cards**; Zara kept angling for a Labubu.

### "The nicest part of my day" · ~13:20–15:00
We stopped moving. Ajay took the kids to find a place to play while Candice shopped (then rotated). Lying on something looking up: **"I'm really enjoying being on here. This is the nicest part of my day. So please don't ruin it for me by rushing to toys."** Imaginative play, a dollhouse display, a kids' zip-line area. (Ajay also spent part of this stretch dictating Albee product notes — capture-safety copy, renaming the home screen to "Albee 1," test requirements — work folded quietly into the rest.)

### Food court & Pokémon shirts · ~15:00–16:00
Grazing again rather than a Big Meal — yakitori sticks, octopus, sushi/cucumber rolls, a bakery ham-and-cheese where we reconvened with Candice and Popo. A **Charmander shirt to match Kai's outfit**, a fitting-room stop where we took our shoes off to try things on.

### The hammock & the clouds · ~16:00–17:30
The afternoon's heart. A spot Ajay found just by wandering — *"How did you find this spot?" "We walked around."* A **hammock**, catch with the Charizard bottle in the wind, and a long lie-down watching clouds:

> *"Can you tell me if you find any cool shaped clouds?" … "that's like a croissant" … "kind of like a watermelon slice" … "I like doing this with you." "I like doing this too."*

And the family rule said plainly to a kid: **"An adult has to be happy too. Papa has to be happy too. No one's happy if any one of us are not happy."**

### Sunset, lava, dinner hunt, home · ~17:30–20:11
Golden hour. A roaring floor-is-lava / samurai game ("Dodge! Lava! Run!"), a sunset caught from an elevator window (*"Wow, look at the sunset from here, guys"*), and the now-familiar dinner negotiation: **Candice — "No, I'm not up for ramen, there's nothing I'd want"** (the pork/ramen problem again), and a firm no to Kai's cup-noodle plan. Home to wind down. *"I like the slow travel. I just want to sit in one spot."*

## What we learned about how *we* travel

Carry these into Days 4+:

1. **One anchor + white space beats a packed list.** We dropped the Skytree deck, the aquarium, and the entire Ikebukuro evening — and the day got *better*. The playground, the hammock, the clouds were the highlights and none were on the plan. Plan one must-do per day; protect big unscheduled blocks on purpose.
2. **Turn transit into a game.** Pokémon GO walked all five of us to the Skytree with zero complaints. "They forget they're walking." Have a walking game ready for any stretch over ~10 minutes.
3. **Hands-on food > famous food (for the kids).** Make-your-own onigiri thrilled them more than any institution would have. Look for *do-it-yourself / interactive* meals over reservations.
4. **Graze, don't commit.** We ate well without one big sit-down — konbini breakfast, a fruit-market snack, food-court sticks and rolls, a bakery reconvene. Flexible grazing fits the kids' energy better than booked meals.
5. **Rotate the adults; an adult gets to be happy too.** Ajay takes the kids to play while Candice shops, then swap. Said explicitly: "no one's happy if any one of us isn't." Build a rest-rotation into each afternoon.
6. **Find a home-base rest spot and let the kids play out.** A bench, a grassy patch, a hammock — pick a spot, let them run, and don't move until someone's ready. The day's calm came from staying put.
7. **Toys are regulation tools.** The Charizard bottle and new Pokémon toys carried hours of catch, lava games, and cooperative play. Let them bring the play currency.
8. **Mornings need meltdown margin.** Kai's morning explosion and the running Zara-bathroom friction are predictable. Build slack into the first two hours; don't schedule anything tight before ~10:00.
