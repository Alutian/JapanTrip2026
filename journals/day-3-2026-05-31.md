# Day 3 — Sunday, May 31, 2026

### Asakusa → Skytree Town · the day we stopped rushing

*Tokyo. Family of five: Ajay, Candice, Zara (9), Kai (7), Popo (75). Reflected on from the hotel, ~20:30 JST, reconstructed from Albee captures across the day.*

---

## At a glance

The plan was a packed East-Tokyo arc: Senso-ji → Kappabashi → up the Skytree deck → Sumida Aquarium → train to Ikebukuro → biggest Pokémon Center → Sky Circus. We did maybe a third of it, and it was the best day yet.

What actually happened: a slow hotel morning, Senso-ji and omikuji fortunes, then we let the kids loose at a riverside playground, made our own onigiri for lunch, turned the walk to the Skytree into a Pokémon-GO hunt, found the Pokémon Store inside Skytree Town, and then mostly... stopped. Ajay found a spot to lie down and said out loud, *"this is the nicest part of my day — please don't ruin it by rushing to toys."* We watched clouds, played catch with a Charizard bottle, found a hammock, and let the afternoon be quiet. We never went up the Skytree, never made it to the aquarium, and never went to Ikebukuro. Nobody minded.

**The lesson of the day, in one line:** the itinerary is a menu, not a checklist — and the unscheduled hours were the ones we'll remember.

## The day, the way it happened

### Slow hotel morning · ~05:41–06:40
We woke up slowly. Packing the day-pack — three battery packs ("Albee running in my backpack all day"), passport into the locked pocket, Zara's hat, sunblock. Kai on the potty narrating. A Pixel buzzing at 6am like a phantom alarm. Strawberry milk turned into a "secret potion." There'd been a small argument the night before, named and patched gently in the morning ("I just want to tell you the truth… that's okay, I know").

Then the safe code — *"6-9-6-6-2-2, okay, let's go"* — one last check (*"what is this strawberry milk, are we taking it?"*), and the door. The kickoff line, half to the room and half to the recorder: **"Day 3. Starting out."** A beat later, small voices stacking down the hall, already amazed by nothing in particular: *"Oh my gosh. Oh my gosh. Oh my gosh."*

### Out the door · ~06:40–07:50
First stop was cash and a konbini breakfast — chocolate croissants and a hot latte; the kids rejected the breakfast sushi. At Yotsuya the Suica situation resolved itself only partway: **three working cards, one dead.** We tapped through on the working ones and decided to sort the broken card later rather than detour. Onto the Marunouchi line, transfer toward the Ginza line, kids chanting *"Let's go!"* the whole platform. Ajay pulled up the trip app on the train — *"I built this app just for Japan"* — and previewed Asakusa for them.

### Asakusa & Senso-ji · ~07:50–09:00
We arrived early enough to beat the buses. The morning had teeth in it before it had wonder.

**The lost-toy battle.** Kai had left his water-sprayer toy on the train — the second toy he'd lost. Ajay tried to make it a lesson and it escalated: *"You grab things, you run to a spot, you put it down, and you forget about it. That's why I wanted to get you a necklace with it — so you won't lose it."* Kai heard judgment instead of help — *"you're saying I'm the worst"* — and the spiral started. Ajay held a firm line (*"we're not getting another replacement"*) and walked him through a repeat-after-me reset on the dirty ground: *"Can you say it calmly? 'I'm gonna try my best to be responsible for my toys.'"* It didn't land cleanly the first few times (*"no, now you're being angry — try again"*). This is the friction that fed what came next.

**The shame talk.** Kai had drawn something inward — guilt over the night before, over a wish he'd made — and he spiraled: *"My wish is the worst wish ever."* Ajay didn't argue him out of it; he reframed it. *"You're feeling shame and guilt, and you're feeling bad. But those are feelings that are good feelings. I know they feel really bad — but they're good feelings. You know why? Because you're a good person. You feel bad about things that were bad. If you weren't a good person, you wouldn't care."* And then, leveling himself with the boy: *"I feel that a lot too, okay, when I make mistakes."* He walked Kai to a bench — *"let's pick a nice bench to sit on"* — settled him on the smooth (not wet) rocks, and slid into a made-up bedtime-style story to carry him out of it: *"Little Timmy's in adventures. It's gonna get interesting, don't worry, Little Timmy."* By the end Kai was asking about the fortune sticks. The reset worked.

**The Earth talk.** Walking up toward the temple, Ajay told the kids where everything came from — the Big Bang, a star exploding, dust pulled into clumps by gravity, clumps big enough to condense and make heat becoming stars, smaller ones becoming planets, and Earth *"just got very lucky because it's far away enough from the sun."* Kai, reaching: *"What if we fly onto the star?"* — *"The sun is very hot. We'd melt."* — *"Like on the other side of it?"* And then the kid landed somewhere bigger than the question: *"Oh, it goes forever. It doesn't stop forever and ever. It's infinite."* Ajay let it sit, then turned it toward keeping the day: *"Hey Kai, do you want to take a picture — not of yourself — of anything you see right now, just to remember that you were here?"* A minute later, the quiet companion line that's been rattling around since: **"because you're not going to remember you were here when you were bigger, because your memory will change."** (Which is, of course, exactly why we record these at all.)

**Omikuji at Senso-ji.** Drop a coin, shake the box, draw a stick, match the number to the drawer. There was a small comic tangle — Kai read the stick as "98," but the stick *gives* you a drawer number (it was 2), and they had to hunt it down: *"91, 81, 71, 51, 21 — oh, two, right here."* The fortune itself read like the day's own horoscope, written in advance:

> *"The moon is covered by floating clouds. You will have difficulties in making any plans. Do your best and ask for help from others. Then you'll be able to catch a good fortune. You don't have to worry. Open your eyes and look into the future. Your wishes will be realized in the end."*

Floating clouds, plans you don't get to keep, and it works out anyway — we'd spend the whole afternoon lying under those clouds. The clap-clap-bow, the curiosity about the temple ropes (*"I wonder if the birds don't go up there and poop on the paint"*), Nakamise stalls, the golden roof (*"where's the gold tower?"*). Ajay split off briefly for cash and circled back.

### The riverside playground · ~09:00–10:00
Here's where the plan got abandoned in the best way. Instead of Kappabashi, we made for the river — *"the river, I want to see a river"* — with a pacing reminder along the way: *"you have to pace yourself all day, heel to toe."*

The morning's first clean win was a **vending machine**. Ajay coached the kids through tapping a Suica to buy their own apple juice — *"now take this card, not that one… did it work?"* — and the payoff was total: **"Hey! It worked! Can I have one too?"** *"Success, we've got apple juice. I'm happy with at least some vending machines."* (And a very Ajay aside, hunting a recycling bin but refusing to detour for it: *"next time we see a vending machine there'll be recycling next to it… as long as it's on the way, I'm not going out of the way."*)

Then he set the home base out loud, the way he does — telling the kids exactly where to find him: *"I want you guys to know where I'm gonna be. I'm gonna go up there to the wall and look at the river. If you need me, I'll be up there."* **Bumper boats** (*"it's not in the river, it's in the pool"*), an obstacle course, climbing. On the monkey bars he turned it into a coached skill — *"arms like this… squeeze your butt in, that'll help you go up… I'm gonna hold you the whole time."* And the sweetest invented game of the morning, a hug toll that inflated by the second: *"you gotta hug me for 10 minutes"* → **"100 minutes of hugs! I get hugs. I like hugs."** A scraped shin (*"it did actually hurt"* — shaken off), the recurring Zara-bathroom negotiation. Pure unstructured kid joy.

### Make-your-own onigiri · ~10:00–11:50
Lunch beat the plan, too. We skipped the sukiyaki institution and found a **make-your-own-onigiri restaurant**. *"You get to make your own rice ball — does that sound pretty fun?"* Salmon or chicken sets, the round of orders (*"salmon or chicken? salmon or chicken?"*), the whole family routed to the sink first — *"everybody's gonna wash your hands, soap and water."*

The quiet teaching moment was the miso soup. Ajay wanted the kids to taste it, and made it about respect rather than rules: *"we're in a restaurant where they made the soup by hand, and they are artists. You know what artists are? They work really hard to make it really delicious. This is not the normal restaurant."* Then a cheers, and a soft off-ramp so it never became a fight: *"You don't have to drink it. I just want you to try it. I'm not forcing you. Just try it."* Kai tried, didn't love it, and afterward said *"I wish I tried"* — and Ajay caught it: **"You did try. Thank you for trying."** A small, exact bit of grace.

Candice, settling in: *"I don't like crowds — we eat different places every time,"* and the restaurant nearly empty, *"so it's just us. Private."* Kai, unprompted, the line of the meal: **"I love this restaurant."** Everyone ate together, which was the whole point. (Ajay also mused aloud about a product idea — restaurants always want a review; what if there were a better way to capture the good experiences.)

### Pokémon-GO walk to the Skytree · ~11:50–12:10
Light rain. To get five people walking happily toward the Skytree with no complaints, we opened Pokémon GO and narrated the whole way — *"tap the rhino… did you catch him or did he escape? Okay, the stronger ones you might have to use a stronger ball."* The trick was that the game only advances when you walk, so the walking happened by itself: *"you have to keep walking, buddy"* … *"walk on the left — left, left, left — you're gonna get hit by bikes."* A Fletchling went down on the first throw and Ajay made it an event: *"it usually shakes three times — it only shook once. Very strong."* **"They forget that they're walking."** Best transit hack of the trip so far.

### Skytree Town / Solamachi · ~12:10–13:20
Not the observation deck — the base. A little fruit-and-snack market (figs, watermelon, snow crab samples, iced lattes; *"we have to buy something so we can sit here"*). Then the toy floors, hunting for the Pokémon Store across the mall — and the payoff, pure delight in his voice: **"That's the Pokémon Store! See, I told you — when you find it, you're gonna find it!"** *"Wow, buddy, look at this place!"*

He made it fair, too: *"Hey sweetie, you can pick something too, okay?"* to Zara — with the older-sibling caveat, *"nothing too big, it's got to be the same size as ours so you can play with her."* The practical-dad layer underneath the magic: hunting for the figures with key-wings *"so you can attach them to your backpack so we don't lose them."* Kai went back and forth between a Squirtle and a Pokéball-with-a-stamp-inside, landed on a **Baby Charizard** and **Pokémon cards**; Zara kept angling for a Labubu.

### "The nicest part of my day" · ~13:20–15:00
We stopped moving. Ajay took the kids to find a place to play while Candice shopped (then rotated). Lying back, looking up, the kids already tugging toward the next purchase, he said it plainly — half boundary, half confession: **"I'm really enjoying being on here. This is the nicest part of my day. So please don't ruin it for me by rushing to toys. You guys got toys already. You'll get another one — but I want to enjoy this."** Imaginative play, a dollhouse display, a kids' zip-line area. (Ajay also spent part of this stretch dictating Albee product notes — capture-safety copy, renaming the home screen to "Albee 1," test requirements — work folded quietly into the rest.)

### Food court & Pokémon shirts · ~15:00–16:00
Grazing again rather than a Big Meal — yakitori sticks, octopus, sushi/cucumber rolls, a bakery ham-and-cheese where we reconvened with Candice and Popo. A **Charmander shirt to match Kai's outfit**, a fitting-room stop where we took our shoes off to try things on.

Somewhere on an escalator between floors, Ajay said the thing out loud that turned out to be the day's operating principle — to a kid, but really to everyone: **"An adult has to be happy too. Papa has to be happy too. No one's happy if any one of us are not happy."** It rolled straight into an escalator-safety story (a girl who almost fell, *"she held on to this thing on the other side"*) and then, gently, into how he moves through the world: *"You notice how I walk around the house? Mom says, 'you never make any sounds.' Because I walk quietly — I don't hurt my feet. It should feel soft, like you're a cat. Cats walk really quietly."* A toucan in a pet display, a parrot, and on we went.

### The hammock & the clouds · ~16:00–17:30
The afternoon's heart. A spot Ajay found just by wandering, and the kids couldn't believe it: *"This is a really nice spot. It's a great spot. It's the **best** spot. How did you find this spot?"* — **"We walked around. I said I want to look around for the next place to sit and we can play."** The method was the message: there was no spot on the map, he just kept moving until one felt right.

A **hammock**, catch with the Charizard bottle in the wind, and a long lie-down watching clouds together — the cloud game stretching out, both of them naming shapes into the windy sky:

> *"Can you tell me if you find any cool shaped clouds?" — "that's like a croissant" — "actually, kind of like a watermelon slice, like you cut a watermelon" — "I can also see like a raindrop"… "Like a butter. You spread the butter."*
>
> **"I like doing this with you." "I like doing this too."**

It was in this quiet, mid-cloud, that Candice said the thing we'll watch tomorrow for — softly, almost folded into the small talk about apple juice and fruit cups: *"I think I'm a little bit afraid Momo's getting sick."* The shopping and resting kept rotating around it; nobody made it a crisis, but it's noted.

### Sunset, lava, dinner hunt, home · ~17:30–20:11
Golden hour, and Ajay said out loud what the whole day had been arguing for: **"I like the slow travel. I just want to sit in one spot."** He even talked himself through *why* the base beat the deck: *"It's better than being in the tower. I mean, in the tower you can see the sunset — but I'd rather be here, see the change, run around. Inside it's just three floors of that stuff; out here it's more freedom. There's more to explore."* The unplanned little café he'd spotted earlier: *"that was key."*

There was strategy underneath the calm, too — the dessert held in reserve as a tool, not a bribe: *"when they're crashing a little bit later, then I'll give them a treat. I'll cheer them up."*

Then the kids detonated into a roaring **floor-is-lava / samurai game** — a wall of *"Dodge! Dodge! Wrong side, there's lava there!"*, climbing for an imaginary summit (*"we're cast all the way up there, so we gotta climb up"*), and a running rule between siblings: *"you're Jet, not the baby."* Pure golden-hour imagination. A sunset caught from an elevator window (*"Wow, look at the sunset from here, guys"*) — and a small scare to end it, *"I think Popo might be lost. Where's Popo? Mama's gonna find her."* (She did.)

Then the now-familiar dinner negotiation: **Candice — "No, I'm not up for ramen, there's nothing I'd want"** (the pork/ramen problem again), and a firm no to Kai's cup-noodle plan. Home to wind down.

## What we learned about how *we* travel

Carry these into Days 4+:

1. **One anchor + white space beats a packed list.** We dropped the Skytree deck, the aquarium, and the entire Ikebukuro evening — and the day got *better*. The playground, the hammock, the clouds were the highlights and none were on the plan. Plan one must-do per day; protect big unscheduled blocks on purpose.
2. **Turn transit into a game.** Pokémon GO walked all five of us to the Skytree with zero complaints. "They forget they're walking." Have a walking game ready for any stretch over ~10 minutes.
3. **Hands-on food > famous food (for the kids).** Make-your-own onigiri thrilled them more than any institution would have. Look for *do-it-yourself / interactive* meals over reservations.
4. **Graze, don't commit.** We ate well without one big sit-down — konbini breakfast, a fruit-market snack, food-court sticks and rolls, a bakery reconvene. Flexible grazing fits the kids' energy better than booked meals.
5. **Rotate the adults; an adult gets to be happy too.** Ajay takes the kids to play while Candice shops, then swap. Said explicitly: "no one's happy if any one of us isn't." Build a rest-rotation into each afternoon.
6. **Find a home-base rest spot and let the kids play out.** A bench, a grassy patch, a hammock — pick a spot, let them run, and don't move until someone's ready. The day's calm came from staying put.
7. **Toys are regulation tools.** The Charizard bottle and new Pokémon toys carried hours of catch, lava games, and cooperative play. Let them bring the play currency.
8. **Mornings need meltdown margin.** Kai's morning explosion and the running Zara-bathroom friction are predictable. Build slack into the first two hours; don't schedule anything tight before ~10:00.
