# Day 4 — Monday, June 1, 2026

### Odaiba — the hard day, and the gift that turned it around

*Tokyo, last full day before Kyoto. Family of five: Ajay, Candice, Zara (9), Kai (7), Popo (75). Reflected on from the hotel, ~21:48 JST, reconstructed from Albee captures across the day.*

---

## At a glance

The plan was an Odaiba day built around **Pokémon GO Fest** — out to the bay, a science museum (Miraikan), teamLab in the afternoon, the giant Gundam. On paper, a lot.

What actually happened: GO Fest turned out to be **locked to ticket-holders** — the one thing the day was anchored on quietly fell out from under us before 10am. We hit a brutal **rush-hour train crush** that scared the kids, burned an hour on **ATMs and a dead IC card**, and spent the morning refereeing nonstop sibling competition. This was the hardest day of the trip — Ajay said out loud more than once that he needed "a total reset," and Kai, at his lowest, said *"I wish I was a really good dad."*

And then it turned. Ajay took Kai on a **secret mission** to buy Zara the Labubu she'd wanted since Day 3 — and framed it so *Kai* was the one giving it. teamLab caught fire with the kids ("the water" was everyone's favorite). And after a dinner hunt that nearly collapsed (*"I give up, I don't know how to solve this"*), we landed at a **sushi counter** that became one of the best meals of the trip. The walk home turned into a block-by-block Pokémon hunt under the streetlights.

**The lesson of the day, in one line:** when the anchor breaks and everyone's fraying, the day isn't saved by the plan — it's saved by one generous, deliberate act that resets the whole mood.

## The day, the way it happened

### Slow, logistics-heavy start · ~06:31–07:50
The morning began as a planning session, not an adventure: confirming the hotel could hold packages, the Hakone free pass, Stitch reservations, an ATM run at the 7-Eleven that *didn't* work (*"I think there's something around my card — you should try yours"*). Breakfast, brushing teeth, the small frictions. And an honest flash of how heavy the day already felt, said straight to Candice: **"all the planning we're doing… none of the things today are things that I would do on my own. They're all things that are fun for you guys — and in the meantime you're just complaining."**

The Day-3 lost-toy lesson came back as a hard rule for the Charizard: *"When you put something down — anything, including Charizard — it doesn't go down ever. It goes in my backpack. That's why I'm carrying a backpack. If it goes to the floor, it's getting lost, and you don't get it back."* (Kai had already left his hat at a restaurant the day before.) "In your words — where does your Charizard go when you're not holding it?" — **"In the backpack." "Yes, thank you."**

### Out the door, into rush hour · ~07:50–08:50
A teaching beat on the way: how to say good morning — *"ohayo"* — and the discovery that **Candice knows some Japanese** (her Taiwanese grandparents spoke it: *"I can count to ten"*). Then the day's first real wall: **rush hour.** Three trains, a packed Ginza-line car, and the kids overwhelmed and frightened in the crush. Ajay held a scared kid against the crowd — **"I got you, buddy. I got you."** — and absorbed the meltdown: *"I want to go to the hotel. I want to go home." "Home where? California?"* He named the lesson in real time: *"This is the only time it's gonna be like this. We're not gonna rush our travel again — that's why you have to get out earlier."* And reframed the fear: *"We didn't almost die. They do this every day. It's just a new experience for you."*

Underneath it all, a long **ATM / IC-card saga** — the dead Suica from Day 3 finally swapped for replacement PASMO cards, but only after multiple failed machines, cash splits, and Ajay muttering the truest logistics line of the trip: *"This is adding a lot of time and friction to our trip."*

### The Yurikamome to the bay · ~08:50–09:10
The mood lifted the second we hit the **driverless Yurikamome** — front seats, no driver, the city sliding by. *"The train has tires, not rails — it's like a bus!"* Pokémon GO came out (storage already full: *"we have too many Pokémon"*), the big golden Fuji-TV ball across the water (*"it's like a big Pokéball"*), a pirate-ship stadium, cranes loading boats.

### Odaiba beach & the GO Fest bust · ~09:10–10:00
First, a small perfect exchange by the water. *"Kai means ocean."* — *"Do you like the ocean?" "I love the ocean." "That means you love me." "I love you."* Ice cream (cookies-and-cream, strawberry, rainbow birthday cake) that immediately became **"the stuff of mama's nightmares"** — Zara wearing it down her hand, leg, and shoes (*"you didn't even notice?"*). The Statue of Liberty replica (*"it's real, but it's small — the real one's in New York"*).

And then the gut-punch: **Pokémon GO Fest was a ticketed event.** The thing we'd come for was locked. Nothing loaded, everyone was playing at once, and a long stretch went to fiddling — transferring Zubats, combining, reading Mega-Evolution explainers, even recording a "teach us Pokémon GO" voice memo. Ajay improvised a sharing system that became the day's quiet rule: **"three catches each — you hold the phone, you stay together, you hold hands, then you switch."**

### Miraikan, the science museum · ~10:00–13:00
Getting in was the low point of the morning. Kai dug in outside — *"Why do I always have to go? Fine, you can stay outside, I'm going in"* — and the friction boiled over: **"I'm very upset at you right now, because you're complaining again, just like you did yesterday at the temple."** A real teaching line landed in the middle of it: *"Being a leader doesn't mean being first."* And then, honestly, **"I need a total reset."**

But the museum delivered once we were inside — the space-station toilet (*"you pee in the tube, you poop in the box"*), the tilted ISS floor, a CO₂-cycle wall, an algorithm dance-floor puzzle with logic gates (X's and H's), quantum computers, a bonsai that "tells your fortune," and a society exhibit that produced a genuinely tender beat: *"there are 15 people in your life where, if they left — not family, not close friends — you'd still feel very sad."* Outside, the giant **Gundam statue** we waited on to "move," which barely did: *"That was boring. Yeah — just okay. Well, at least we got to see some of it."* Lunch in the cafeteria was the usual negotiation (no donuts, Kai face-deep in spaghetti) — but it ended on *"thank you for getting water, buddy. I love you. That's so nice of you."*

### The secret Labubu mission · ~12:00–13:18
This is the heart of the day. In the middle of the cafeteria, Ajay made a quiet decision and pitched it to Kai: **"You want to do something really nice for you and Zara? We can go buy the Labubu — and you can give it to Zara as a present."** He told Candice they were "going back to the museum," and slipped off to the mall with Kai.

What followed was a master class in raising a kid. Kai worried about the cover story — *"it feels like we're lying"* — and Ajay leveled with him: *"sometimes you tell a little bit of a lie to do a good thing for somebody, and you tell them the truth later — that's okay."* Walking the hot pavement, Kai said the thing that breaks your heart: **"I wish I was a really good dad. I wish I was like a papa." "Next time I'm gonna try my best."** At Pop Mart they found the Labubu; at the Gundam store Kai chose a robot for himself, and Ajay tied the two together: **"You know why you're getting this? Because you're being generous. Because you said you wanted to go get her a toy." "When you're generous, I want to be generous." "Thank you, Papa. I love you. I love when you're generous."**

Then he coached the *delivery* — show up empty-handed, ask Zara about her time first, *listen*, then reveal: *"give her her toy first — because if you show her your toy first, she'd say 'not fair, how come he got a toy?'"* And the reunion paid off every step: **"They got you a love-oo-boo." … "I got the love one because I love you." … "He said it was his idea to get you a present. Thanks, Kai."**

### teamLab · ~14:20–18:00
The afternoon's redemption, after a card-trouble train ride back (the dead card biting again at the gates). teamLab was sensory wonder: the **water room** (a warm river you wade barefoot — *"it's a foot massage… rub your toes"*), the infinite mirrored crystal and **flower universe** (*"floating in the falling universe of flowers"*), long-exposure photo rooms, colored-sphere climbing. The **balloon/ball room** was pure joy — *"keep it uppy! keep it uppy!"* — until Kai got swallowed by the crowd and pushed around and came out crying. Ajay owned it: **"I shouldn't have left you alone. Next time, Albee with you. No one's gonna hurt you if I'm with you."**

The runaway hit was the **catch-the-animals forest** — an AR net that's basically Pokémon GO with whales and tigers. Kai was *gone*: **"I'm just into catching now."** "Papa, I caught an orca!" When everyone was asked their favorite at the end, it was unanimous: **"the water, the water, the water."**

### The dinner that almost broke him · ~18:00–19:10
Home base was a bus ride away (another crowded one — *"those rules don't apply when rush hour happens"*), and then the recurring monster: dinner. Food courts sold out or closed, sweets everywhere and nothing real, Kai melting, Ajay hitting bottom out loud: **"I don't know. I give up. I give up. I don't know what to do. I don't know how to solve this."** He took a breath, walked it off, and found it — a **sushi counter at Ginza Six (Itamae Sushi)**, the whole family at the bar, shrimp tempura for the kids.

### The best meal of the trip · ~19:10–19:48
It turned into a feast and a bonding hour. Kai waited patiently and Ajay caught it: **"thank you for being so good while you're waiting. I know you're hungry. You're doing really good. I'm proud of you."** Ajay ran a running sushi ranking with the kids — **"number three is my favorite, number two second, number one third"** — the fatty tuna *"ridiculously good,"* the eel (*"that's the same fish you had yesterday and loved"*), a California roll, the kids' tempura. About eighty dollars for five at a real sushi bar: *"it's crazy how good and cheap the food is here."* A genuine high after the day's lows.

### Pokémon under the streetlights · ~19:48–21:48
The walk home became the best version of the whole day. Block by block, the three of them ran the cooperative system — spin, tap, pass, take turns — chasing a near-impossible bird (**"that was the hardest one ever!"**) and a 1,200-power monster, and Ajay folding a values lesson into the chaos: **"we're mad at the bird, not at Zara, okay? We have to be supportive of our team."** Home for the shower rotation (*"you're number one, you didn't shower last night"*), cup-of-noodles, a bedtime grapefruit Kai chose himself — *"why'd you pick this one?"* — **"that's a really good reason, buddy. I love that."** And a last, small, beautiful question from a kid winding down: *"how can I get sick if I eat from the same bowl as you?"*

## What we learned about how *we* travel

Carry these into Kyoto and beyond:

1. **Never travel at rush hour with the kids.** The morning crush genuinely scared them and poisoned the first three hours. Said in the moment: *"we're not gonna rush our travel again — that's why you have to get out earlier."* Be on trains before ~8:00 or after ~9:30, never in the peak.
2. **Don't anchor a whole day on one ticketed/uncertain thing.** GO Fest being locked pulled the floor out. (Same shape as Day 3 — the difference is Day 3 had white space to fall back on and this day didn't.) Confirm access *before* building the day around it, and keep a real fallback.
3. **Pre-lock a Candice-safe dinner — this is now non-negotiable, three days running.** Tonight nearly collapsed (*"I give up"*) before the sushi-counter save. The win took an hour of searching and a lot of fraying. **Conveyor/à-la-carte sushi and shrimp tempura is a proven family hit** — bank it. Lock the dinner spot in the morning.
4. **One deliberate generous act resets a bad day.** The Labubu surprise — and making Kai the *giver* — flipped the entire afternoon. When competition and complaining spiral, a planned act of generosity beats more discipline.
5. **Transit-as-game scales to a whole day.** Pokémon GO carried the bay walk, the museum walks, and especially the night walk home. The turn-taking rule (*"three catches each, hold hands, switch"*) kept it cooperative instead of a fight. Have it loaded and have the rule ready.
6. **Interactive/AR experiences are the kids' favorite mode.** teamLab's catch-the-animals and the water room beat every static exhibit; *"I'm just into catching now."* Bias the plan toward hands-on, body-in-it experiences over look-don't-touch.
7. **Sibling competition is the day's real tax — separate and rotate early.** The fighting drove most of the hard moments. Ajay's fixes worked: split the kids between adults, give each a *job* (navigator, phone-holder), and rotate. Build that in from the morning, not after it blows up.
8. **Ajay carries the toys now.** The Day-3 lost-toy rule held: everything lives in the backpack. It works — but it means one adult is the pack mule all day. Factor that into who rests when.
